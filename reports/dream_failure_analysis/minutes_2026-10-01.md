# Minutes: what we found about the dream-trained driver, 1 October 2026

Working notes for writing up the technical note (`reports/technical_note.md`) once more
experiments are done. Nothing here has been put in the note yet. Every number comes from a file
in this folder; the README has the full setup and reproduction commands for each test.

## The question

The dream-trained controller (`runs/dream_v3`) scores a mean of 251.7 (sd 184.8) on the 100 test
tracks, against 908.4 (sd 68.0) for the controller trained in the simulator. Where and why does
it fail in the simulator? Everything below is about that one controller and the one world model
it was trained in (`runs/mdnrnn`), on test seeds 1,000,000 to 1,000,099.

## Where we started (hypotheses from the discussion)

1. The driver copes with easy stretches and fails when the track gets bendy.
2. In the simulator it spins off at a sharp turn and does not recover, while the dream shows a
   stable car on the road (seen once, in `reports/dream_v3_side.gif`, track 1,000,000).
3. Idea for a fix: train the controller in the dream on selected track segments, using the track
   boundaries (inner and outer lines). Not built; see "Candidate next experiments".

## What was found, in the order it was found

**1. It fails everywhere, not on the bendy tracks.** Return against track bendiness: correlation
-0.09 (p = 0.36) with total turning, -0.10 (p = 0.32) with sharpest turn, -0.17 (p = 0.10) with
length. Returns are low on all tracks: median 188, quarter-points 124 and 336, best 767.
(`bendiness_corr.json`.) Hypothesis 1 is not supported.

**2. It leaves the road early on every track, and not at sharp bends.** (`bend_check.json`.)

| | Dream-trained | Simulator-trained |
|---|---|---|
| Tracks where the car leaves the road for 20+ steps | 100 of 100 | 11 of 100 |
| Median step of the first departure | 194.5 | 394 |
| Departures it never recovers from | 41 | 2 |
| Median share of tiles collected | 29% | 100% |
| Sharpness of the bend where it leaves (percentile within the track, 0.5 = chance) | 0.43 | 0.60 |
| Departures in the sharpest quarter of bends (25 expected) | 19 of 100 (p = 0.20) | 2 of 11 |

Hypothesis 1 is again not supported: the departures are not concentrated at sharp bends. Not
tested: whether the car fails at the first corner of each track whatever its sharpness.

**3. The GIF anomaly was real on that track.** (`dream_v3_side_filmstrip.png`.) Up to about step 200
the dream and simulator agree (predicted score 201.8, real 219.1 at step 201). Then the real car
spins off and its score goes flat (235.0, 227.0, 233.0 at steps 241, 321, 361) while the dream car
stays on the road and its predicted score keeps climbing (259.0, 367.5, 421.4). Values read from the
rendered frames. One track only.

**4. Given the real actions, the dream follows the car off the road, but blurred.**
(`dream_fidelity.json`.) The dream was started 30 steps before the real car first leaves the road and
fed the real recorded actions. A classifier that reads "on road" from an image encoding (held-out
area under the curve 0.996, 97% accurate, catches 97.5% of off-road frames) gave, over the steps where
the real car is off the road: 0.10 from the real frames, 0.32 from the dream. Median difference +0.22
(95% interval 0.18 to 0.27), positive on 91 of 100 tracks. The dream is also less sure on the road
(0.69 against 0.89), so its signal is blurred in both directions: it keeps about 42% of the real
on-road versus off-road contrast (post-hoc measure; area under the curve 0.79 against 0.99). It
shows the car leaving the road at some point in all 100 windows, and pays slightly less reward than
reality (median 31.1 against 40.0).

**5. When the controller steers inside the dream, the dream shows the car on the road.**
(`dream_closed_loop.json`, `closed_loop_frames.png`.) Same start, but the dream now starts in exactly
the real state (the controller's first action matches the real one to 5e-7), and the controller
chooses the actions from the dream's own images and memory. Over the same steps (where the real car
is off the road):

| Probability the car is on the road | Real frames | Dream, real actions | Dream, controller steers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean | 0.10 | 0.32 | **0.87** (0.90 after the VAE check) |
| Share of those steps the dream says "on road" | n/a | 22% | 96% |

Controller-steered minus real: median +0.83 (95% interval 0.78 to 0.85), positive on 100 of 100
tracks; controller-steered minus replay: +0.56, positive on 99 of 100. The dream pays the controller a
median of 133 over the window, against 32 when replaying the real actions and 40 in the real run.
The controller drives differently in the dream: mean throttle 0.64 against 0.22 in the real run over the
same steps, mean steering difference 0.62. In the pictures a straight road keeps reappearing under the
imagined car where the real frames show grass. The VAE check (decode then encode each imagined latent
before reading it) leaves the result unchanged, so it is not the classifier misreading dream images.

**6. The dream is wrong about the controller's own actions.** (`dream_actions_in_sim.json`.) The
actions the controller chose inside the dream (4 futures per track) were run in the real simulator
from the same real state (rebuilt by replaying the real history; all 400 rebuilds matched the log
exactly). Under those actions the real car is on the road for 35% of the window and 11% of its last 20
steps; the dream says 0.89 and 0.90. Dream minus real, over the steps where the real car is off the
road: median +0.82 (95% interval 0.80 to 0.83), positive on 100 of 100 tracks. So the explanation
"the controller just chooses different actions that would have worked in the simulator" is ruled out.

## Conclusion so far

- The dream is accurate for actions like those in its training data (test 4: it follows the real
  car off the road, if blurred).
- For the actions the controller discovers, it shows the car staying on the road when the real car
  leaves it (tests 5 and 6, 100 of 100 tracks).
- The search is therefore training the controller in a world that is too forgiving, so it does not
  transfer. This is the controller exploiting errors in the model, not a general blur and not a
  weakness at sharp bends.

Evidence status: the pattern is measured. *Why* the dream is wrong there is not tested. Candidate
cause: the training data is mostly on-road driving, so spins and recoveries are rare.

## What this changes in the technical note (for when we write it up)

- **Title**: the current one ("Why controllers trained inside a learned world model fail on
  CarRacing-v3: a diagnosis") no longer fits what the evidence shows. The finding is specific:
  the model is exploitable by the controller. (Earlier title candidates and the IET guide rules are
  in the conversation; the title must be precise, informative, in plain words, and not assert more than
  was measured.)
- **Appendix A.3 and section 3.5** say the model gets the consequences of control input right and
  rule out "road regenerated beneath the vehicle regardless of input". That test only held constant inputs
  from a mid-track state. Tests 5 and 6 contradict it for controller-chosen actions and should be
  reconciled in the text.
- **Table 3.2 / section 3.3**: the cause is currently given as "closed-loop fidelity" by elimination.
  Tests 5 and 6 make it direct: for the same actions the dream and simulator disagree. The five rejected
  candidates still stand; the "sharp bends" idea is a sixth, also rejected (tests 1 and 2).
- **Summary conclusion** ("the repaired model separates competent from incompetent driving") and
  conclusion 4.5 need rewording in light of this.
- The rank-correlation material (already revised on 30 September) is consistent with this: the model
  does not rank controllers reliably.

## Caveats on everything above

One controller, one world model, one temperature (1.15), one set of 100 test tracks; one seed per
configuration, as elsewhere in the note. The on-road reading is from a classifier on image
encodings (checked by the VAE round trip, not by inspecting every frame). In test 6 the dream's
actions are applied open-loop in the simulator, which shows the dream's prediction for those actions
is wrong, not what the controller would achieve on real frames. The "first-corner" and "recovery from
an off-road start" questions are untested. The metrics in tests 4 to 6 were fixed before running;
the two measures marked post hoc in test 4 were added afterwards.

## Candidate next experiments (not decided)

1. Start the dream from a point already off the road: can the controller recover there, in the dream
   and in the simulator?
2. Test the candidate cause directly: how many frames in the training data are off the road or
   mid-spin (classifier on `data/latents.npz`)?
3. Does the same hold for the first world model and its dream-trained controller (`wm_dream_v1`), and
   for the repaired model with other seeds?
4. Fixes that follow from the finding: make the dream aware of the road boundary (an on-road signal
   from the track lines, used as a penalty or to end the dream), or train the controller in the dream
   from segments chosen to include departures. Needs per-frame car position (replay the recorded actions
   in the simulator) if boundaries are to be used.
5. Whether the failure sits at the first corner of each track (needs absolute bend sharpness, which
   `bend_check.py` did not save).
